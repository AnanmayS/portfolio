# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The primary audience is peers, recruiters, and internship hiring teams. Peers should be able to browse casually and get a sense of personality, interests, and what Ananmay is building. Recruiters and hiring teams should quickly understand technical range, project quality, and readiness for software engineering or internship opportunities.

## Product Purpose

This portfolio presents Ananmay Som Singh as a computer engineering student who builds useful software across machine learning, backend systems, DevOps, data analytics, and games. It should feel like a personal corner of the web rather than a rewritten resume: easy to scan, casually memorable, and credible enough for professional review.

Success means visitors can quickly learn who Ananmay is, see the kinds of projects he builds, and find a clear path to GitHub, LinkedIn, or email. The site should reward a little exploration with cool details, motion, and visual touches without making the content harder to read.

## Brand Personality

Personal, quiet, simple, and technically curious. The tone should feel approachable and hand-built, with enough polish to signal care. Cool features and visuals are welcome when they serve the identity: compact interactions, subtle motion, playful project previews, and small moments that make the page feel alive.

## Anti-references

Avoid resume-template portfolio pages, corporate career-site layouts, and pages that simply restate resume bullets. Avoid generic dark developer-site patterns, especially oversized hero sections, predictable black-and-neon palettes, and project cards that could belong to anyone. The design should not feel loud, overproduced, or like a flashy startup landing page.

## Design Principles

1. Make the site feel personal before it feels polished.
2. Let projects show capability through concrete, inspectable details rather than resume-style claims.
3. Keep the page quiet and simple, then add motion or visuals only where they create delight or clarity.
4. Preserve fast scanning for recruiters while leaving casual visitors small things to discover.
5. Treat the site as a living personal artifact, not a static credential sheet.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use readable contrast, keyboard-friendly navigation, clear focus states, and simple language. Motion is part of the intended experience, but animations should be purposeful, lightweight, and compatible with reduced-motion preferences. Interactive controls should have accessible labels, and core content should remain understandable without relying on video, animation, or color alone.
